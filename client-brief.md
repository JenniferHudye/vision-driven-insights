### Voice and style rules (from Jennifer Hudye Voice/Tone Guide, verbatim intent)

- Write at a 5th to 6th grade reading level. Anyone should be able to follow it.
- Moderately informal. Enthusiastic and authoritative. Playful. Lots of metaphors and word-pictures.
- Inspirational but practical: get people excited, then give them the steps.
- Write like she talks. If it doesn't sound natural said out loud, it's not her.
- One sentence per paragraph, unless stacking short fragments on purpose.
- Use ellipses liberally when trailing a thought. Occasionally start a new line with an ellipsis.
- End sentences on the strongest word. End sections on the strongest sentence.
- Lead into a statement with a question.
- Progressive sentence stacking: each short line adds real context, not fake drama.
- Long-to-short cadence for rhythm. Action-verb stacking for momentum. Periods instead of commas for a breathless pace.
- Explain any jargon (EBITDA, KPIs, DNS, schema) the first time it appears.
- American spelling. Emphasize with ALL CAPS words, bold, italics. Underline model/framework titles. "$1 million" can be "$1M".
- Keep it PG. At most one masked curse per piece, and only if warranted.
- No hype, no overwhelm, no manipulative urgency.
- After drafting, read it out loud. Ask of each line: does this attract, repel, or neutralize? If it repels or neutralizes, cut it.

## 3. POV, Frameworks & Pillars

- **Distinctive POV (one sentence):** Most planning either fixes your business or your personal life, but rarely both at once, and that's exactly why it never sticks: when you fuse personal clarity and business strategy into one vision, every decision starts moving you toward the life AND the business you actually want.
- **Named frameworks (for each: name, definition, principle, when used):**

  1. **Vivid Vision** (also "the Vivid Vision Framework")
     - *Definition:* A detailed, present-tense picture of your business and life three years out, written as if it has already happened, using pictures, language, and storytelling.
     - *Principle:* People and teams move toward a picture they can see. Vague goals don't pull anyone forward. A vivid, specific future does.
     - *When used:* When a founder is scaling, hiring, raising money, or feeling scattered, and needs the whole team rowing toward the same future.

  2. **The Vision Driven OS**
     - *Definition:* A single operating system that fuses personal clarity and strategic business planning into one process, instead of treating them as two separate exercises.
     - *Principle:* Business plans and life plans that are built apart from each other pull the founder in two directions. Built together, they compound.
     - *When used:* When a founder keeps hitting revenue targets but not the lifestyle he built the business for.

  3. **The Vivid Vision Method / The Vision Driven Journey** (the proven process)
     - *Definition:* The step-by-step path Vision Driven runs clients through: Vivid Vision Strategy Call, Vivid Vision Mind Map, Vision Expander, Copy Creation, Vivid Vision Board, and Vivid Vision Rollout.
     - *Principle:* A vision only works if it's captured, written well, made visual, and rolled out to the team. Each step exists so the vision doesn't die in a notebook.
     - *When used:* Start to finish of a Vivid Vision engagement.

  4. **The Golden Jail Cell vs. The Golden Compass**
     - *Definition:* The Golden Jail Cell is a business that's too big to walk away from but too dependent on you to give you freedom. The Golden Compass is the opposite: you always know where you're headed because you're guided by your own vision and values.
     - *Principle:* Without a vision of your own, you end up playing someone else's game and trying to win it. With one, you play your own game.
     - *When used:* Naming the exact trap a stuck-but-successful founder is in, and the way out.

  5. **The Big Life Stages of Entrepreneurship**
     - *Definition:* The arc founders move through, from an "achievement-driven" stage (chasing the next milestone) to a "vision-driven" stage (leading from a clear picture of the life and business you want).
     - *Principle:* Most founders never consciously graduate from achievement-driven to vision-driven, so they keep score with numbers that never feel like enough.
     - *When used:* Helping a founder see where he is now and what the next stage actually looks like.

  6. **Vision Amplifier** (Vision Amplifier Breathwork)
     - *Definition:* A 15-minute daily guided breathwork practice for connecting to your vision and staying in a creative, expansive state.
     - *Principle:* A vision you only look at once a year doesn't change your decisions. A daily practice keeps it live.
     - *When used:* Ongoing, after the Vivid Vision is built.
